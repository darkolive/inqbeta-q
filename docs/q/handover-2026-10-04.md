# Handover — 4 October 2026

## Why we're doing this

Darren, at the end of the day:

> "If we can solve this off grid locally, while still being able to benefit
> from being accessing globally, we've got a full resilience model. And that
> gives any operation host incentive to buy kit … for us as Dark Olive to
> make training videos and run courses. It just becomes a spawning ecosystem
> of stimulus, which is what everybody needs: something positive to focus on
> and make something that works, and is going to help us not be miserable
> doing what you have to do just to pay the bills, which are too high."

The shape of it: **locally**, the device first, a van or mini PC as the hub,
neighbours carrying for each other; **globally**, clouds, kept storage at
nodes, federations in different places as separate fates; **bridged by
receipts**: nothing is let go until it's held somewhere else. Hosts buy kit
because it earns; carriers earn by helping; backers get rewards they use;
Dark Olive teaches the skills the ecosystem needs. Free to give, never free
to take (the orchid).

## Decided today (ADRs)

- **ADR-Q-030 — The network market.** Members add a federation as one of
  their places (Join); providers run nodes from a *Network provider* plugin
  with a provider card (stated vs proven); federations post *wanted* offers
  when their heat passes a threshold, accepted by rules; contracts as
  available or reserved; Nebula certificates for the contract's term;
  draining before leaving; price as a band (providers' average cost to
  commercial prices) chosen by the federation's governance, with a price
  index in the directory; hot and cold vaults (the device is the hottest
  place; the only gap is what you made offline, shown plainly); spare space
  and carriers (§10). Kit replacement is the operator's own choice.
- **ADR-Q-031 — Crowdfunding in credits, following, the incubator as a
  test bed.** Gift campaigns and backing with rewards in kind (never shares
  or money back with more on top: that's FCA territory); three kinds —
  running costs, all or nothing, start when there's enough — with pledges
  held as commitments (released if it falls short); following on the home
  page; enterprises start in credits and go live when ready; sponsorship
  through the mint.
- **ADR-Q-027 additions — the reserve ratio.** Buying and cashing out don't
  move it; it falls only when the reserve is drawn on; below the host's
  minimum (e.g. 20%) cash-outs pause, buying never does and heals it.
  Checking the reserve: *Confirmation of Funds* (the bank says yes/no, never
  the balance). **Four levels of trust**: stated, honoured (cash-out
  record), witnessed (a treasurer signs), bank-confirmed. A penny with the
  mint's id proves whose account; a screenshot proves nothing.

## For the tax and legal conversation

From HMRC's own manuals (4 October): a members' club isn't taxed on surplus
from dealings with full members (mutual trading, BIM24205); but non-cash
receipts of a *trade* are taxable when they're money's worth, i.e.
convertible or transferable (BIM40051), so a member running storage as a
business may owe tax on credits before cashing out. The VAT threshold is
£90,000. Grants of credits to a business may be income. Equity or returns to
the public are FCA-regulated. All for the accountant and solicitor before
real money moves.
