## What we learned

### About the design (written up in `showing-money.md`)

1. **Pictures carry the meaning; numbers confirm it.** Every number comes
   with words; one sentence says the state and what to do.
2. **Colour means one thing everywhere**: primary is in, error is out,
   warning is held; success → warning → error is health.
3. **Health is "enough", not "more"**: the battery is empty at the line you
   can't cross and full at twice it. Speedometers and dials were rejected.
4. **Charts are running totals by the clock**: zero is the floor when values
   are near it, gridlines are round numbers, committed is a band, and you step
   through time a window at a time.
5. **Every number opens its receipt.** A line you can't open is only a claim.
6. **Quiet type, plain shapes**: nothing "coiny", no bold rows, a breadcrumb
   instead of a page header.
7. **Lists open directly under what opened them**, before the chart.

### About organisations

8. **A federation is a bank** in the old drovers' sense: a holding house that
   issues notes backed by its books. Its reputation is its books.
9. **Committed has two sources**: an agreement holds credits, or a rule
   holds them (a grant).
10. **Office, not person.** Buttons that speak for an organisation name an
    office; whoever holds it this term answers.
11. **Power comes from a role you've taken up**, never from being you. One
    hat at a time, declared, recorded; out of role, peace and quiet.
12. **Working for an organisation is a receipt between you both**, and the
    business card is drawn from it. It's not a tax registration, but you can
    pass on the organisation's references without holding them.

### About the way of working

13. **Edit on the Mac directly** (`device_bash` with Python), not by
    committing a staged copy: `device_commit_files` can resend a stale cached
    copy for the same path. If a staged copy has to be sent, use a new name.
14. **svelte-check doesn't run on the Mac's Linux shell** (rollup's arm64
    module is missing). Svelte files were checked with the Svelte compiler
    and TypeScript with `tsc`; neither does the full check a production
    build does.
15. **Darren can't push from here** (no SSH from the shell; no GitHub from the
    cloud). He pushes from Terminal.

## Decision: what's next (5 October, evening)

All of today's ADRs (034–040) and both handovers were weighed against each other.

**Next: build the door (ADR-Q-034), end to end. Then Offices (ADR-Q-007 §5–6).**

### Why the door first

1. **It's the one real risk today.** inqbeta.com answers anyone who makes a
   passkey (audit A1). Everything else on the list is a feature; this is the
   lock.
2. **It's ready.** It's decided, it depends on nothing, and its six build steps
   are already written.
3. **It isn't a detour.** `isLetIn` is the first check, made at every server
   door, of *who may act*: a receipt signed by the root, by a linked key or by
   a pass the root gave. Offices and in-role receipts are the same shape: a
   mandate from the federation key, checked at the same doors. Building the
   door builds the groundwork for both.
4. **It can be proved whole** in the test rig, in one session: Darren let in,
   Tess refused, given a pass and let in, the pass revoked, refused again; on
   .dev, everything plays and nothing reaches a server.

### Why Offices straight after

Five ADRs now wait on it: 037 (ask the office), 038 steps 2–8 (acting in role,
including §8, the federation's account), 039 (working for an organisation)
and 040 (the day photo: stewards act in role). One piece of foundation
unblocks four decisions.

### Then, in order

3. In-role receipts, checked by the servers (038 step 2), closing audit A2.
4. Ask the office (037 steps 1–2).
5. The federation's account in role (038 §8), with the lock on cash-outs (A4)
   built into it, because both touch the same cash-out path.

### Not next, and why

- **Story engine pictures**: nothing blocks them, but nothing is at risk
  either. They're a good palate-cleanser between keystones.
- **Grants (036), engagements (039), the day photo (040)**: they need Offices
  first, and legal, employment or data protection advice before they're real.
- **Responsive pass and the checks (A5–A7)**: done *inside* the door session,
  not separately. The test rig and Playwright at phone size come up anyway,
  and the "Opening soon" screen is the first thing to check at both sizes.
  Fix the decks TypeScript errors at the start, so new errors show.
